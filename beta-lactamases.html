The chemistry

Breaking the ring

All β-lactams share a four-membered β-lactam ring that mimics the end of the peptidoglycan building block and binds the PBPs. β-lactamases attack the ring’s carbonyl, using a serine (classes A, C and D) or zinc ions (class B), and water then opens it. The opened drug cannot bind PBPs.

β-lactamase inhibitors (clavulanate, tazobactam, avibactam, vaborbactam, relebactam) are taken up by the enzyme instead, protecting the partner β-lactam. None of the licensed inhibitors blocks the zinc enzymes.

Ambler classes

Ambler groups β-lactamases by their amino-acid sequence into four classes. The Bush–Jacoby system groups them by what they destroy and what inhibits them (group 1 = AmpC, group 2 = class A and D, group 3 = metallo). Tap a class.

ESBL, AmpC and CPE

Class A

ESBL

Extended-spectrum β-lactamases destroy penicillins, third-generation cephalosporins and aztreonam, but are inhibited by clavulanate. In the UK most are CTX-M-15 in E. coli and K. pneumoniae, often on plasmids with OXA-1 and other genes that also give co-amoxiclav, piperacillin–tazobactam, quinolone and aminoglycoside resistance.

Spot it
Cefotaxime, ceftriaxone or ceftazidime MIC >1 mg/L, cefoxitin susceptible, with clavulanate synergy on a combination disc or strip test
Serious
A carbapenem (meropenem, or ertapenem if stable). In MERINO, 30-day mortality was 12.3% with piperacillin–tazobactam vs 3.7% with meropenem for ceftriaxone-resistant E. coli and Klebsiella bacteraemia. IDSA advises against piperacillin–tazobactam and cefepime even if reported susceptible, except for cystitis
Oral
Step down by susceptibility (co-trimoxazole, ciprofloxacin). Cystitis: nitrofurantoin, fosfomycin or pivmecillinam
Sparing
Temocillin IV for urinary infections, including bacteraemia from a urinary source

Class C

AmpC

Chromosomal AmpC is normally switched off but is induced by β-lactams. Mutants that make it all the time (derepressed) can be selected during treatment: an isolate susceptible to ceftriaxone at first becomes resistant (about 1 in 5 Enterobacter bacteraemias treated with third-generation cephalosporins in older series). Plasmid AmpC (CMY, DHA) occurs in E. coli and Klebsiella.

Who
Moderate to high risk of derepression: Enterobacter cloacae complex, Klebsiella aerogenes, Citrobacter freundii. Lower risk: Serratia, Morganella, Providencia, Hafnia. Also Pseudomonas
Spot it
Third-generation cephalosporin and cefoxitin (MIC >8 mg/L) resistant, no clavulanate synergy, cefepime usually susceptible; confirm with a cloxacillin or boronic acid test
Treat
For the moderate–high-risk organisms avoid ceftriaxone for serious infection even if reported susceptible. Cefepime if the MIC is ≤1 mg/L; a carbapenem if higher or an ESBL is also present. Non-β-lactams by susceptibility: co-trimoxazole, quinolones, aminoglycosides

Classes A, B and D

CPE

Carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales. The “big five” are KPC (A), OXA-48-like (D) and the metallo-β-lactamases NDM, VIM and IMP (B). In England OXA-48-like and NDM are the most often reported. They are carried on plasmids that spread between species and between patients.

Spot it
Meropenem MIC above the EUCAST screening cut-off of 0.125 mg/L; high-level temocillin resistance suggests OXA-48. The laboratory names the enzyme by lateral flow test or PCR and can refer to UKHSA
Treat
Depends on the enzyme. KPC: ceftazidime–avibactam, meropenem–vaborbactam or imipenem–relebactam. OXA-48: ceftazidime–avibactam. MBL: aztreonam–avibactam, or ceftazidime–avibactam plus aztreonam. Cefiderocol is an alternative. Always discuss with microbiology
Control
Single room with contact precautions, flag the record, and screen contacts and at-risk admissions (rectal swab) as in the UKHSA CPE framework

Which β-lactams each enzyme destroys

Enterobacterales, by enzyme on its own. Real isolates often carry several (ESBL with OXA-1, or a carbapenemase with an ESBL), so always go by the isolate’s reported susceptibilities. Carbapenems against OXA-48 and aztreonam against MBLs show as variable because co-carried enzymes usually decide the result.

Patterns point the way; confirmation is by the laboratory’s phenotypic and molecular tests.

Key points

  • Inoculum effect: with a high bacterial load, MICs of piperacillin–tazobactam and cefepime against ESBL producers rise, one reason they fail in serious infection.
  • Gram-positives: the S. aureus penicillinase (blaZ) is why benzylpenicillin rarely works; MRSA resistance is PBP2a, which no β-lactamase inhibitor helps.
  • Non-fermenters: Pseudomonas combines AmpC, efflux and porin (OprD) loss; Acinetobacter baumannii carries OXA-23-like carbapenemases; Stenotrophomonas maltophilia has its own metallo-β-lactamase (L1), so it is intrinsically carbapenem resistant.
  • Anaerobes: Bacteroides fragilis makes a cephalosporinase (so co-amoxiclav, piperacillin–tazobactam and carbapenems are used); a few carry the CfiA metallo-β-lactamase.
  • Non-β-lactam options depend on the isolate: aminoglycosides, fosfomycin, co-trimoxazole, quinolones, tigecycline and colistin.
