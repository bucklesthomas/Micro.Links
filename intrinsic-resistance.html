Reference

Intrinsic resistance

Antibiotics each organism is expected to be resistant to, whatever the laboratory result. Look up by organism, or by antibiotic to see which organisms it will not cover. Acquired β-lactamases (ESBL, AmpC, CPE) are explained on the β-lactamases page.

Checked November 2026Main source: EUCAST Expected Resistant Phenotypes v1.2

Only intrinsic (expected) resistance is shown. An antibiotic not listed is not necessarily active: acquired resistance is common, so always use the isolate’s susceptibility results. AmpC high risk of clinically significant chromosomal AmpC; AmpC AmpC present but low risk (IDSA 2024).

  • Quinolones: EUCAST also lists nalidixic acid, an old (first-generation) quinolone with no Gram-positive activity, so all Gram-positives are intrinsically resistant to it. It is not used in UK practice, so it is left out here; labs may still use a nalidixic acid disc to screen H. influenzae and M. catarrhalis for fluoroquinolone resistance. No common pathogen is intrinsically resistant to levofloxacin, so its column is usually empty.

Source: EUCAST Expected Resistant Phenotypes, version 1.2 (2023), as encoded in the open-source AMR package, with notes from the EUCAST footnotes. AmpC risk groups from the IDSA 2024 guidance on antimicrobial-resistant Gram-negative infections.
