Streptococcal species and endocarditis risk

Type the species from a positive blood culture to see how often it turns out to be infective endocarditis, its risk group, and whether to request TTE or TOE.

Checked November 2026Main source: Deas et al. eClinicalMedicine 2025

Population figures from adults with streptococcal bacteraemia. They do not replace clinical assessment, Duke-ISCVID criteria or advice from cardiology and the endocarditis team.

Notes

  • Risk means the proportion of patients with bacteraemia due to that species who were diagnosed with endocarditis. Across all streptococci it was 7.25% (1,028 cases in 14,183 isolates).
  • The "small five" (S. mutans, S. cristatus, S. gordonii, S. sanguinis, S. gallolyticus) cause about 8% of streptococcal bacteraemias but nearly 40% of streptococcal endocarditis.
  • S. mitis/oralis carries a lower risk per episode (12%) but is the commonest streptococcus in endocarditis, because it is so common in blood cultures.
  • Species figures come from the 2025 meta-analysis where it reports them, and otherwise from the Danish registry studies it built on. Species marked "group" have only a risk group (no separate figure) in those sources.
  • Echocardiography advice follows the Danish flowchart (risk group, number of positive bottles, cardiac risk factors). It has not been validated prospectively.

Sources: Deas G, Lee TC, Colston J, Albur M, Vasant J, Nobbs AH, Williams P, Hamilton F. Streptococcal endocarditis: a meta-analysis of species dependant risk. eClinicalMedicine 2025;87:103425 (full text); Chamat-Hedemand S et al. Prevalence of infective endocarditis in streptococcal bloodstream infections is dependent on streptococcal species. Circulation 2020;142:720–30; Chamat-Hedemand S et al. Proposal for the use of echocardiography in bloodstream infections due to different streptococcal species. BMC Infect Dis 2021;21:689.
